## Outcome

`crates/ti4-review/src/view.rs` gains three named things and one big move:

- **`BoardLayout { rect, size, scale, center, radius }` + `BoardLayout::new`** — where the board goes,
  including the 0.45/1.2 scale clamp and the 72 px the Fracture takes out of the ring.
- **`tile_point(layout, tile)`** — the axial-ring / Fracture-band / Nexus-corner placement, verbatim
  from the reviewer.
- **`fracture_shown(tiles)`** — the Fracture's presence read from the tiles rather than the frame, so
  the painter needs one input instead of two.
- **`draw_board(painter, response, layout, tiles) -> Option<String>`** — every stroke, returning the
  tile the pointer chose.

`ReviewApp::board()` is now 28 lines: header row, legend, `allocate_painter`, `BoardLayout::new`,
`board_view`, `draw_board`, and one assignment. `gui.rs` went from 1,986 to 1,753 lines; it no longer
imports `Align2`, `FontId`, `Pos2`, `Shape`, `Stroke`, `Vec2`, `hex_corners`, `planet_offset`,
`draw_wormhole`, `draw_fracture_portal` or `draw_unit_symbol`.

Meaning (`board_view`), position (`BoardLayout`) and strokes (`draw_board`) are now three separable
ideas, and the middle one became testable for the first time — it used to be four local variables
inside a private method.

## How R01's output was shown unchanged

The move is mechanical and both halves were proved against `HEAD` rather than asserted.

**Proof 1 — `tile_point` is the moved position formula.** Extract the `let point = match
tile.special_area…` block from `HEAD:crates/ti4-review/src/gui.rs`, apply two renames
(`response.rect` → `layout.rect`, `scale` → `layout.scale`, `center.` → `layout.center.`), strip
`let point = `, normalise whitespace and trailing commas:

```text
PROOF 1  tile_point == moved position formula: True (390 chars)
```

**Proof 2 — `draw_board`'s body is the moved painter code.** Extract `HEAD` lines 1679-1687 (the
Fracture band title) and 1691-1912 (the tile loop), dedent, apply the renames and the five call-shape
edits below, then compare with the current body between `let mut selected` and `selected`:

```text
PROOF 2  draw_board body == moved painter code: True (4915 normalised chars)
```

Normalisation collapses whitespace and drops a comma directly before `)`/`]`/`}` — rustfmt added one
such comma (`Pos2::new(…, …, )`). Nothing else differs across 4,915 characters.

The complete list of adaptations, which is the whole surface a reviewer needs to check:

| # | Change | Sites | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `response.rect` → `layout.rect` | 3 | the rectangle is a layout field now |
| 2 | `scale` → `layout.scale` | many | same |
| 3 | `radius` → `layout.radius` | 2 | same |
| 4 | `center.` → `layout.center.` | 2 | same |
| 5 | the position `match` → `tile_point(layout, tile)` | 1 | lifted so it is nameable and testable; body moved verbatim (Proof 1) |
| 6 | `self.selected_tile = Some(tile.system.clone())` → `selected = Some(…)` + returned | 1 | the painter must not own either app's selection; each caller assigns its own |
| 7 | `if fracture_visible` → `if fracture_shown(tiles)` | 1 | the painter takes tiles, not the frame; equivalence is tested, not assumed |
| 8 | `&painter` → `painter` | 5 | the parameter is already `&Painter` |

**Operation inventory.** The moved span makes 19 `painter.*` calls; `draw_board` makes 19. No stroke
was added, dropped, or reordered — the loop is the same loop.

**The one thing that is not a rename** is #7, so it is tested rather than promised: across the setup
frame and twelve advanced frames of a real review, `fracture_shown(board_view(…))` equals
`frame.state.fracture_in_play`; and because this example session never brings the Fracture in, the
positive case is forced by flipping the flag on a cloned frame and requiring the tile to appear.

## The 6 tests (`crates/ti4-review/tests/board_layout.rs`)

| Test | What it pins |
| --- | --- |
| `the_scale_tracks_the_window_and_clamps_at_both_ends` | 1 px/unit at the design size, half the window is half the board, the 0.45 floor and 1.2 ceiling both bite, and the shorter axis wins |
| `the_fracture_buys_its_band_out_of_the_ring` | the ring lifts by exactly 72 px, sideways by nothing, and the Fracture does not shrink the board |
| `ring_tiles_sit_on_the_axial_grid` | (0,0) is the centre; 126 along q; 108 plus a 63 skew along r; diagonal neighbours 125.032 - the projection is 0.8 % squished and that is now a pinned fact, not an accident of one app |
| `the_two_special_areas_get_their_own_corners` | Fracture centred on its band, Nexus lower-left, and `board_view` spells both areas exactly as the painter matches them (a typo on either side used to be invisible) |
| `the_painter_and_the_frame_agree_about_the_fracture` | `fracture_shown` == `fracture_in_play` over 13 real frames, plus the forced positive case |
| `a_wider_window_moves_the_board_not_the_meaning` | 37 tiles on the example map; every ring tile's displacement scales exactly; the two special areas stay anchored to the window edges instead |

Three of my own assumptions were wrong and the tests said so before the code did: r-step is 63 px of
skew (I guessed 54), the example map is 37 visible tiles (I guessed 52), and diagonal spacing is not
equal to row spacing (so "six equidistant neighbours" is false in this projection and was replaced by
pinning both numbers). That is the value of making geometry testable, which is what this package
buys.

## Judgement calls

- **The painter returns the clicked system** instead of writing a field. Both apps keep their own
  selection state and share every pixel; the alternative was a callback, which is the same thing with
  more types.
- **`BoardLayout` keeps both `rect` and `size`.** R01 computed `scale` from `ui.available_size()` and
  the centre from `response.rect`, and egui can trim one without the other. Faithful beats tidy: the
  two inputs are kept separate and the difference is documented on the fields.
- **`fracture_shown` reads the tiles.** It removes the frame from the painter's inputs; the risk (the
  tile list and the flag disagreeing) is exactly what the fifth test attacks.
- **`Selected` is a `TileFill` variant, not a stroke here** — unchanged from R02-006b.
- **Not extracted in this package:** `decision_panel`, `player_panel`, the timeline and the top bar.
  The replayer's own layout (plan §R02-007) wants a *choice* panel, a branch selector and rebuild
  progress beside the timeline, so wrapping those would have generalised R01's widgets to fit an app
  that does not use them that way. They stay in R01; R02-007b composes its panels from `view::`
  models and primitives, which R02-006c already provides.
